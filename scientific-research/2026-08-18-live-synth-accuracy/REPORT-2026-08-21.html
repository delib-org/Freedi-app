From 0.067 to 0.932: Making a Live Deliberation-Synthesis Pipeline Accurate in Two Languages

The complete record of the accuracy programme — every defect found, every fix measured, and the effect on speed and cost
Freedi live-synthesis research series  ·  2026-08-18 → 2026-08-21  ·  Study root: scientific-research/2026-08-18-live-synth-accuracy/  ·  Prepared by Claude (Anthropic) for Tal Yaron  ·  All cited runs certified against build fingerprints; nothing in this report is deployed.
Abstract. Freedi's live synthesis pipeline clusters community proposals in real time: near-duplicate statements merge into a single synthesized proposal, and syntheses are filed under topic headings. A 100-statement benchmark with known ground truth (10 topics × 5 ideas × 2 paraphrases, English and Hebrew corpora) scores the pipeline as 0.6·F1synth + 0.4·F1topic. At the start of this programme the pipeline scored 0.067 (English) and 0.066 (Hebrew): a single topic cluster absorbed all 100 statements and zero syntheses formed, while the one healthy-looking metric (coverage 100/100) hid total failure. Over four days and sixteen measured interventions, English reached a certified band of 0.884–0.910 across three arrival-order seeds with an exact synthesis layer (150/150 ground-truth pairs, zero false merges), and Hebrew — after per-question migration to a stronger embedding model and the removal of every judgement-free placement path — reached 0.932, the highest certified run in the study. The published text was audited separately: every one of 98 member statements audited in a live run remains recognisably present in the text that replaced it, and scope-inflation (text that silently widens what people agree to) was driven from 43/50 syntheses to 0/50. The programme's unifying law: embedding distance may propose; only an LLM judge may decide. Net operating impact: per-statement wall time unchanged in English (33.8 s → 33.8 s under identical pacing), ≈+1 fast-tier LLM call per statement (≈+8–15% LLM cost, concentrated in the cheap tier), partially offset by a fix that eliminated ~144 wasted judge calls per day per settled question.

1  Background: the system and the instrument

The system under test is runSinglePipeline, fired for every created option (liveSynthOnOptionCreate). For each arriving statement it decides: attach to an existing synthesis, spawn a new synthesis with a partner (LLM-written unified proposal), file under a topic heading, queue for admin review, or wait. The output is a three-level structure — raw statements → syntheses → topic headings — that participants browse and evaluate.

The instrument is a frozen benchmark: one civic question, 100 statements constructed as 10 topics × 5 ideas × 2 near-paraphrases, in English and a structurally identical Hebrew translation. Statements are fed one at a time in a seeded shuffle into Firebase emulators running the compiled functions build; the harness waits for the pipeline to settle after each, then exports the full cluster structure and audit trail. Score = 0.6·F1synth + 0.4·F1topic, pairwise over all C(100,2) statement pairs, so it degrades smoothly and an improvement loop can see progress. A run is certified only if the scorer's self-test passes, the functions/lib build fingerprint is byte-identical before and after, and no rebuild occurred mid-run. Uncertified runs are reported but never cited.

2.2  Discipline, purchased by the programme's own mistakes

  1. Validate the instrument before trusting its numbers. Both scorers carry self-tests that must pass first.
  2. Never re-implement code under test. Two early measurement bugs each inverted a conclusion (Finding 8): a hand-copied re-judge pump silently stopped matching the real code, and the settle detector mistook emulator silence for completion. Every later offline A/B drives the compiled artifact.
  3. Replay on the states the system actually meets. An offline replay on tidy final theme sets predicted an improvement that regressed live by 0.045 (Finding 13) — the live sweep faces ~19 half-formed headings, not 11 settled ones. The change was reverted and the replay methodology retired.
  4. Pre-register decision criteria. The filing-prompt selection rule and the spawn-judge fix criteria were fixed in writing before the deciding measurements ran, so results could not bend the rules.
  5. Certify builds. One early run was invalidated when the emulator hot-reloaded seven times mid-run; every certified run since pins the build fingerprint before and after.

3  What was fixed — the complete list

Phase I — making the pipeline work at all (2026-08-18/19; English 0.067 → 0.910)

  1. The topic-cluster black hole (Finding 1; 8d1aadffc). At defaults, the first topic cluster absorbed every later statement: 80% of English (100% of Hebrew) cross-topic pairs cleared the 0.60 attach gate, because the shared "Question:…\nAnswer:…" embedding prefix lifts the whole cosine floor — and a clustered option was then excluded from ever merging with its twin. Zero syntheses formed. Fix: topic attaches require centroid cohesion with the cluster as a whole (a max-over-members signal cannot tell "same theme" from "same question"), and cluster membership no longer forecloses synthesis.
  2. Spawn debounce rescoped and made lossless (Findings 4–5; 8d1aadffc). The 15-second spawn debounce was keyed per question, so one pair's spawn blocked every unrelated pair, and a debounced spawn was silently dropped. Re-keyed per pair; deferred spawns re-enqueue for the queue worker. Later extended: any failed spawn (LLM error, malformed response) re-queues rather than dropping — the measured cost of silent drops was a ground-truth pair lost at cosine 0.898 with no audit row.
  3. Pass ordering: the specific claim gets first refusal (Finding 6). Topic attach ran before synthesis spawn, and a theme that already holds your twin always looks cohesive to you — its centroid contains your twin. In 17 of 23 topic attaches, the member justifying the attach was the statement's own twin; the pipeline used the twin to file the statement away from it. Reordering (synthesis before theming) was worth more than every threshold change combined (0.207 → 0.711).
  4. Member evidence required for attach; snowball brakes. An attach must be justified by closeness to actual member statements, not to an LLM-abstracted title (both observed false merges rode title-only evidence), and a newcomer must be cohesive with a quorum of the cluster, not just one member.
  5. Theme placement became an LLM judgement (Finding 7; 7462ecee3). Measured directly: same-theme synthesis pairs sit at median cosine 0.743 and different-theme at 0.670 — overlapping bands. The best single pairwise cut reaches F1 0.480; a better embedding model 0.535; global clustering to the true k=10 scores 0.432, below greedy attach. Theme membership is not in the geometry at any threshold or model. A fast-tier judge now files each proposal; themes are born from syntheses (not raw-option pairs, which had spawned four near-duplicate headings in one run).
  6. Theme consolidation sweep + cross-synthesis re-judge on members (c9ee88713). Themes are created in arrival order and named after whatever arrived first, so redundant headings accumulate; a periodic judge now merges headings that name the same topic (measured live: 12/13 donors merged same-topic). The duplicate-synthesis gate likewise judges on member statements, not titles — it correctly refused the exact pair ("food-scraps composting" vs "curbside recycling") that had cost precision in two earlier rounds. Result: English 0.910, and the seed sweep (seeds 42/7/1234) certified 0.884–0.910 with the synthesis layer exact on all three: 150/150 pairs, zero false merges in 450 statements.

Phase II — auditing the output and the residuals (2026-08-19/20; the text layer, the review queue, the filing fix)

  1. The published text audited, then fixed (Findings 11, 11b, 14; fb0c0cfb2, 8d10ffd2f). Membership scores ignore what participants actually read. A dedicated judge (validated on known-answer fixtures first) scored every member statement against the text of its synthesis: across 300 members in three runs, zero were lost. The defect it did find was scope inflation — 4 in 50 syntheses silently widened who or what was covered ("freelancers" becoming "residents"), caused by a contradiction in the writing prompt (forbidding invention while demanding "who does what, on what timeline"). Resolving the contradiction with an explicit never-widen-scope rule took blunt fabrication flags from 43/50 to 3/49 and member fidelity to 1.000 (98/98) in a live run.
  2. The review queue stopped lying (Finding 12; c018fd495). Every option queued for admin review across three certified runs — 105 of them — was later placed by the pipeline itself, but the queue rows were never closed: an admin opening it saw a 100% false-positive list that only grew. Placements now resolve their review entries.
  3. A wrong turn, caught and reverted (Finding 13; 5f001e2a2). Showing the consolidation judge each heading's member proposals dominated in offline replay and lost its live run (0.910 → 0.865) by over-merging half-formed headings into a catch-all. Reverted; the methodological lesson (§2.2, rule 3) and one keeper — the judge-once fingerprint, which stops a settled question from re-rolling a non-deterministic merge judge ~144 times a day — survived.
  4. The filing fix (Findings 15–16; df336521c, d576f27f4). Reconstructing the regressed run's exact mid-run states (membership snapshots rescued from the emulator, certified byte-for-byte against the live sweep fingerprint) overturned Finding 13's attribution: the sweep's merges were clean; theme impurity enters at filing, where the judge picked broad-titled "attractor" headings 62% accurately. Two coupled changes: the filing judge sees each heading's contents, and its doubt-bias flips to "when unsure, answer NONE" (a needless heading self-heals via the sweep; a misfile is permanent). Either alone fails — contents under the old bias made misfiling worse (28→37 per 174 replayed decisions); together, 28→17, with the survivors at the corpus's own ambiguity line. Live confirmation: misfiles 12→5, English back in band at 0.902.

Phase III — Hebrew (2026-08-20/21; 0.066 → 0.932)

  1. The model-compatibility guard (f6e16cf30). Vectors from two embedding models are not comparable, and the failure is silent — the cosine is a real number and the wrong neighbours simply rank first. Every read path now honours the per-vector model stamp: a stale vector reads as absent (and regenerates) rather than as a peer. A model switch degrades instead of corrupting.
  2. Per-question embedding model (Finding 17; 5a8d37692). text-embedding-3-small cannot see Hebrew paraphrases (true twin is nearest neighbour 56/100 vs 100/100 English; 3-large lifts Hebrew to 88/100). A question can now pin 3-large while the corpus stays on 3-small: generation, the cache guard, and vector search all resolve the model per question. Migration is one call: reEmbedQuestion({questionId, embeddingModel}).
  3. Bands travel with the model (e3a240562). Thresholds are calibrated to a geometry: under Hebrew 3-large, 4,421 of 4,500 cross-topic pairs cleared the old topic band (producing a 45-member cross-topic mega-theme in the first pinned run) and 234 wrong pairs sat inside the old spawn band. Pinned questions get bands measured for their space (attach 0.86 / spawn 0.80 / candidacy 0.75), written automatically at migration.
  4. Every placement judged — no exceptions (e3a240562, f85df7f12). The judge-free cosine topic-attach (25 of the mega-theme's 45 members) is disabled under LLM theming; and the last geometry-only path — attaching a statement to an existing synthesis — now requires the semantic-equivalence judge's verdict same (verdict-cached, fail-closed), after four false merges entered at cosine 0.899–0.944, above every geometric gate.
  5. Recall in a crowded space (bfff1c00b). Hebrew 3-large packs the whole corpus into 0.64–0.97, so rankings are noisy: a true twin ranked #10 behind four theme-title vectors, and the single top-candidate spawn attempt offered the judge a wrong partner while the right one — one rank below — was never tried. The neighbour window widened 10→15, and the spawn judge now sees up to 3 in-band candidates (a refusal judges one pair, not the statement).
  6. Formulation ≠ intervention (97e3b57dc). The synthesis judge refused genuine Hebrew paraphrases over phrasing deltas ("pocket parks" vs "small green parks"; "per district" vs "per neighbourhood") while English read the same instruction charitably. The prompt now distinguishes formulation differences (merge; keep the most concrete specifics) from intervention differences (refuse). Pre-registered A/B through the compiled function: English exactly unchanged (50/50 merges, 10/10 refusals); Hebrew wrong refusals 5→1; text-safety re-verified (scope inflation still 0/50).
The unifying law, learned defect by defect: geometry proposes, judgement disposes — with no exceptions. In English 3-small space geometry was nearly sufficient, and the judge-free shortcuts were safe by luck of the geometry. Hebrew 3-large compresses the space until distinct ideas are geometrically indistinguishable from paraphrases. Every failure in this programme, in both languages, was a place where a cosine acted without judgement; every fix put a judge in front of one. The converse also held: LLM judgement without the right evidence fails too — the filing judge needed heading contents, the duplicate gate needed member statements, and the consolidation judge, given more evidence than its task could bear, over-merged and was reverted.

4.3  The text layer

Structure scores cannot see whether the published text still says what its members asked for. Measured separately (textFidelity.mjs, judge validated on fixtures first): across 300 member statements in three certified runs, zero members were lost from the text that replaced them; on the final English confirmation run, 98/98 preserved, 0 weakened, fidelity 1.000. The one real text defect — scope inflation, where merged text silently widens who is covered — was traced to a contradiction in the writing prompt and eliminated: blunt fabrication flags 43/50 → 3/49, scope inflation 4/50 → 0/50, re-verified unchanged after every subsequent prompt edit.

4.4  Honest readings

  • Variance: the theme-filing layer contributes ±0.05 run-to-run in both languages (heading titles are generated; early titles steer later filings). English certified band: 0.884–0.910 over three seeds. Hebrew's 0.932 carries one run's filing fortune; the defensible claim is "reliably ≈0.9". A Hebrew seed sweep is the natural next certification.
  • The dips were not noise, and not regressions of their own changes: each Hebrew dip shipped an offline-verified improvement whose composite effect was swamped by a defect fixed later (the unjudged attach path) plus filing variance. The English 0.207 dip was a correct gate exposing the pass-ordering defect.
  • One attribution corrected in the record: the winning Hebrew run never exercised the new attach gate live (judged spawns formed the correct syntheses early, so no wrong attach was proposed); its zero false merges belong to the ensemble, with the gate as unit-tested insurance.
  • English regression check: under the full all-judged build, the English synthesis layer is byte-identical to its certified best (F1 0.990, precision 1.000, zero false merges, 100/100 coverage); the composite difference is filing dice alone.

5  Effect on speed

All pipeline work is asynchronous background processing triggered on statement creation — no user-facing request waits on it. The relevant quantity is time-to-settle per statement: how long until a new statement is fully merged, filed, and downstream recomputation is quiet. The harness measures exactly this; its per-statement wait has a fixed floor of ≈28 s from settle-detection windows, so active pipeline time is the excess above the floor.

The Phase-I changes were latency-neutral or better (gates and ordering reshuffle existing work; the lossless spawn queue trades silent loss for deferred work). The judged paths added in Phases II–III bound their latency by design: the spawn retry spends extra time only after a refusal — which in well-separated geometry is precisely when there is nothing to find — and the attach judge shares a text-keyed verdict cache with the re-judge sweep, so repeated encounters of the same pair are free.

6.2  The cost story of the whole programme

  • The big cost decision was Phase I's, and it bought the accuracy: replacing free-but-wrong cosine decisions (theming, duplicate detection) with judged ones added roughly one fast-tier call per statement and is what moved topic F1 from 0.388 to 0.775+. The heavy tier — synthesis writing — existed before the programme and dominates absolute spend; the programme barely changed its call count in English.
  • Phases II–III added ≈8–15% per-statement LLM cost on top, all fast-tier: contents in filing prompts, ~8–12 filing calls replacing former free attaches, occasional retry spawns, cached attach verdicts.
  • One structural saving offsets much of it: the judge-once fingerprint. Before it, every settled question re-asked the theme-merge judge every 10 minutes forever — ≈144 calls/day/question doing nothing. On a deployment with many settled questions this saving alone plausibly exceeds the judging additions.
  • Embedding-model migration is a rounding error: 3-large costs ≈6.5× 3-small per token ($0.13 vs $0.02 per 1M); at <100 tokens/statement, re-embedding 100,000 statements costs on the order of one dollar. The wider neighbour window adds ≈15 Firestore reads/statement (≈$0.05 per 10,000 statements).
  • Caching does real work: pair verdicts are cached on text (attach gate and re-judge sweep share one cache), the model resolver caches pins for 5 minutes, and the theme sweep's fingerprint means a settled question costs nothing until something actually changes.

7  Threats to validity

  • One corpus, one shape. A single civic question, exactly two paraphrases per idea, no off-topic or adversarial submissions. Every constant is calibrated against this geometry; re-measure before trusting the numbers in a different setting.
  • Composite variance (±0.05 from theme filing) bounds what single runs can prove; the certified English claim is a three-seed band, and Hebrew still awaits its sweep.
  • Benchmark pacing artifacts: the 45 s settle cap censored 2–19 statements per run; the final settle pass captures their results but per-statement attribution near the cap is imprecise.
  • The winning Hebrew run's attach gate is unit-tested insurance, not yet live-exercised.

8  Deployment guidance

  1. Nothing in this report is deployed. Deploy order: npm run deploy:f:test first, then production, as a separate explicit decision.
  2. Questions not migrated are unaffected by the Hebrew machinery — the English regression run is the evidence. Migrating a named question is one call: reEmbedQuestion({questionId, embeddingModel: 'text-embedding-3-large'}) — it pins the model, writes the measured bands, and re-embeds; mid-migration arrivals already use the new model, and the compatibility guard makes the transition degrade rather than corrupt.
  3. Watch during first live migration: vectorSearch.incompatibleModel (transition drop counts) and attach.judgeRefused (the insurance gate's first live exercises).
